SECTION A — PURPOSE AND SCOPE
A.1  This policy sets out how Marhana & Co Consulting Ltd (the "Company") controls the quality of its work: how an engagement is accepted, planned, produced, checked and closed, and what happens when something goes wrong.
A.2  It applies to every engagement, including work performed by Associates, and to every deliverable issued to a client or submitted to a contracting authority on a client's behalf.
A.3  It is structured to follow ISO 9001:2015 so that a contracting authority evaluating the Company's quality arrangements can map it against the clauses of that standard.
SECTION B — QUALITY POLICY STATEMENT
B.1  The Company's purpose is to help small and medium-sized suppliers win public sector work they are capable of delivering. Quality, in that context, means four things:
Principle
What it means in practice
Compliant
The submission meets every mandatory requirement, in the format required, before the deadline. A brilliant response that is rejected on a compliance failure has no value
Accurate
Every factual statement in a response is true and evidenced. The Company does not write claims a client cannot substantiate, whatever the scoring benefit
Deliverable
A commitment drafted by the Company is one the client has confirmed it can perform. Winning on an undeliverable promise is a loss deferred
Useful
The client is left better able to bid next time, through the Evidence Bank, the debrief analysis and the reusable response library

B.2  The Company commits to meeting applicable statutory and regulatory requirements and to the continual improvement of its quality management arrangements.
B.3  The Company gives no guarantee that any tender will be successful. Evaluation is a matter for the contracting authority. Quality is measured on what the Company controls, not on the outcome of an award decision.

SECTION D — LEADERSHIP AND RESPONSIBILITY (cl. 5)
D.1  The board is accountable for the effectiveness of the quality arrangements and for this policy.
D.2  [QUALITY LEAD NAME], [QUALITY LEAD ROLE] is responsible for the day-to-day operation of this policy, for the nonconformity log, and for convening the management review.
D.3  Final quality assurance of a submission may not be performed by the person who drafted it, where the Company has the headcount to avoid that. SECTION F — COMPETENCE AND RESOURCES (cl. 7)
F.1  Competence requirements for each role are set out in the Training and CPD Record, which also holds the mandatory training matrix and the individual CPD logs.
F.2  No person drafts a response in a regulated or safety-critical subject area without either the relevant competence or a named reviewer who holds it.
F.3  Associates are competence-verified before their first engagement and their first piece of work is sampled in full.
F.4  Where the Company does not hold a competence the engagement requires, it says so and either declines the scope or subcontracts it openly. It does not learn at the client's expense without telling the client.

SECTION H — DOCUMENT AND VERSION CONTROL
H.1  Every controlled document carries a document control block showing owner, version, approval, last review and next review. A document without one is not a controlled document and must not be issued.
H.2  There is one master of each controlled document. Where an earlier draft, a variant or a working copy exists, it is moved to an archive location and renamed with the suffix "SUPERSEDED", or deleted. Two live files with the same content and different names is a defect, not a backup.
H.3  Version numbering. Draft versions are 0.1, 0.2 and so on. The first approved issue is 1.0. A change of substance increments the whole number; a correction that does not change meaning increments the decimal.
H.4  Documents issued externally are issued as PDF. The editable source is not issued unless the client needs to work in it, in which case the version issued is recorded.
H.5  Where one document incorporates another by reference — as the Retainer Schedule, the Data Processing Agreement, the Intellectual Property Schedule and the AI and Technology Use Addendum each incorporate the Terms of Engagement — the reference must resolve to exactly one document. The master issue register at Annex C records which that is.
H.6  A document issued to a client is not amended after issue. A correction is issued as a new version with the change identified.
SECTION I — NONCONFORMITY AND CORRECTIVE ACTION (cl. 10)
I.1  A nonconformity is any of the following:
A submission delivered late, or delivered in a form that could not be submitted.
A response rejected by an authority on compliance grounds.
A factual error in a deliverable issued to a client or an authority.
A claim submitted that could not be evidenced.
A clarification deadline missed.
A breach of confidentiality, or a personal data breach.
A conflict of interest identified after work had begun.
A deliverable materially outside the agreed scope, in either direction.
A complaint received under Part C of the Business Conduct Manual.
I.2  Every nonconformity is recorded at Annex A on the day it is identified, whether or not it caused loss and whether or not the client noticed.
I.3  The record must state the immediate correction, the root cause, and the corrective action taken to prevent recurrence. "Human error" is not a root cause; the absence of the control that would have caught the error is.
I.4  Corrective actions are reviewed for effectiveness at the following management review and closed only when the reviewer is satisfied they have worked.
I.5  A nonconformity that engages the professional indemnity policy is notified to insurers in accordance with the policy conditions without delay, and before any admission is made to the client.
SECTION J — CLIENT SATISFACTION AND COMPLAINTS
J.1  A feedback request is sent at the close of every engagement. Responses, including negative ones, are recorded and reported at the management review.
J.2  Complaints are handled under Part C of the Business Conduct Manual and are also logged as nonconformities under Section I.
J.3  Where a client raises a genuine quality complaint about work for which a fee is outstanding, the complaint is resolved before any credit control escalation continues. That rule is repeated in the Credit Control Procedure.

SECTION L — MANAGEMENT REVIEW
L.1  The board reviews the quality arrangements at least [quarterly] and in any event at least annually.
L.2  The standing agenda is: actions from the last review; performance against the objectives at Section E; nonconformities and corrective actions; complaints and client feedback; win and loss outcomes and debrief findings; Associate and supplier performance; competence and training; changes in law, procurement policy or the Company's context; resource adequacy; opportunities for improvement; and the review dates of every controlled document.
L.3  The record at Annex B is the evidence a contracting authority is entitled to ask for. It is completed at the meeting, not reconstructed afterwards.
SECTION M — WHAT THE COMPANY DOES NOT CLAIM
M.1  The Company is not certified to ISO 9001 or to any other quality management standard, and does not hold accreditation from a UKAS-accredited certification body.
M.2  Where a tender question asks whether the Company holds ISO 9001, the answer is no, followed by this policy and the records at Annexes A to C. An aligned but uncertified system, evidenced, scores better than a claim that cannot be substantiated.
M.3  Certification will be considered when the value of contracts requiring it exceeds the cost of obtaining and maintaining it. That assessment is recorded at the annual management review.
