HOW TO USE THIS LIBRARY
Paragraph B.1.1 of the Terms of Engagement is a list of [SERVICE ITEM] placeholders. Every engagement requires that scope to be written. Writing it freehand each time produces inconsistency, and inconsistent scope is where fee disputes start — "I thought that was included" is the single most common argument in professional services, and it is won or lost by what was written before the work began.
For each service line below you will find five blocks. Copy them across:
Scope wording → paragraph B.1.1 of the Terms, or paragraph 1 of the short-form letter
Deliverable → the engagement particulars
Client must supply → paragraph B.1.1.5 and the Information Deadline at A.1.13
Service-specific exclusions → paragraph B.2.1.9, in addition to the standard exclusions at B.2.1.1 to B.2.1.8, which apply to every engagement without exception
Assumptions → include these in the engagement particulars; each one is a variation trigger under B.4.2 if it turns out to be wrong
Two rules. First, never delete the standard exclusions at B.2.1.1 to B.2.1.8 for any engagement — they are the basis on which the fee is calculated and paragraph B.2.2 says so. Second, if a client asks for something not in the scope wording below, it is a variation under B.4.1 and needs quoting before it is done.

1. BID FIT SCORE
Fee band: £150–£350 · Short-form letter suitable: Yes · Personal data involved: Business contacts only
Scope wording
Review of the published procurement documentation for the Opportunity, being the contract notice, specification, evaluation criteria and weightings, and any published clarification responses available at the date of instruction.
Assessment of the Client's stated capability, accreditation status and track record against the published qualification requirements and evaluation criteria.
Assessment of the financial thresholds, contract value, duration and stated risk profile against the Client's stated financial standing.
Assessment of the likely competitive field by reference to publicly available award history for comparable contracts, where available.
Delivery of a written scorecard in the Consultant's standard format, comprising a numerical fit score, a go or no-go recommendation, the strengths the Client should emphasise, and the material gaps requiring attention before submission.
One round of written clarification questions from the Client on the scorecard, answered in writing.
Deliverable: Written scorecard, [●] pages, in PDF.
Client must supply: Capability statement or company overview; list of accreditations held with expiry dates; insurance levels held; three most relevant past contracts with values and client names; latest filed accounts or a statement of turnover; the opportunity reference or portal link.
Service-specific exclusions
The Consultant does not verify any accreditation, insurance level, financial figure or past performance claim supplied by the Client. The fit score is an opinion formed upon the information available at the date of assessment and upon the documentation published at that date. A go recommendation is not an assurance of success and a no-go recommendation is not an assurance of failure. The Consultant does not monitor the Opportunity after delivery of the scorecard and is not responsible for notifying the Client of any subsequent clarification, amendment or extension issued by the Contracting Authority.
Assumptions: The procurement documentation is publicly accessible without portal registration, or the Client supplies it. The Opportunity is a single lot; multiple lots are scored separately and charged separately.

2. BID READINESS ASSESSMENT
Fee band: £250–£500 · Short-form letter suitable: Yes · Personal data involved: Business contacts only, unless personnel records are supplied
Scope wording
Review of the Client's accreditations, certifications, policies, insurances and financial standing against the qualification requirements typically applied in [SECTOR] public procurement.
Review of the Client's existing policy suite against the policy documents commonly required at selection stage, identifying which exist, which are out of date, and which are absent.
Identification of the gaps which would prevent or prejudice qualification, ranked by the likelihood of their being required.
A written readiness report setting out the position, the gaps, the remedial actions required, and an indicative order in which they should be addressed.
One round of written clarification questions from the Client on the report, answered in writing.
Deliverable: Written readiness report, [●] pages, with a gap register in tabular form.
Client must supply: All current policies; all accreditation certificates with expiry dates; insurance schedules; latest filed accounts; organisational structure; details of quality, environmental and health and safety management arrangements.
Service-specific exclusions
The Consultant reviews the documents supplied and does not audit, inspect or verify whether the arrangements they describe are in fact operated. The assessment addresses typical requirements in the stated sector and is not a determination that the Client will qualify for any particular procurement. The Consultant does not draft any policy, obtain any accreditation or effect any remedial action identified, each of which is a separate service quoted separately.
Assumptions: One sector. Assessment against typical rather than named-procurement requirements; assessment against a specific published procurement is a Bid Fit Score and is charged separately.

3. POST-TENDER DEBRIEF ANALYSIS
Fee band: £200–£450 · Short-form letter suitable: Yes · Personal data involved: Business contacts only
Scope wording
Review of the assessment summary or debrief provided by the Contracting Authority, together with the Client's submitted response and the published evaluation criteria and weightings.
Analysis of the score awarded against each criterion and sub-criterion, identifying where marks were lost and, so far as the assessment summary permits, why.
Identification of the evidence, content or capability which would have been required to score more highly against each criterion where marks were lost.
A written improvement plan setting out the findings, ranked by the number of marks recoverable, together with the actions required before the next comparable submission.
One round of written clarification questions from the Client on the plan, answered in writing.
Deliverable: Written improvement plan, [●] pages, with a criterion-by-criterion analysis table.
Client must supply: The full assessment summary or debrief; the submitted response in the form submitted; the published evaluation criteria and weightings; any correspondence with the Contracting Authority about the outcome.
Service-specific exclusions
The Consultant does not advise upon whether any ground of challenge exists, upon the merits or conduct of any procurement challenge, upon any standstill period, or upon any application to the Contracting Authority for further information. Those are matters of legal advice upon which the Client must take independent advice, and the Client is reminded that the period within which a procurement challenge may be brought is short. The Consultant does not correspond with the Contracting Authority on the Client's behalf. The analysis is limited to the information contained in the assessment summary supplied; where the summary is brief or uninformative, the analysis will be correspondingly limited and no reduction in the fee arises.
Assumptions: The assessment summary is provided in full. One submission, one lot.
